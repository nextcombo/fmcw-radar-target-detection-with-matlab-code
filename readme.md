# FMCW Radar Target Detection [with MATLAB Code]

1. Principle of FMCW radar measurement
The FMCW radar architecture studied in this paper is illustrated in Figure 1. Firstly, the modulation voltage generated by the Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC) controls the Voltage Controlled Oscillator (VCO) to produce a linear frequency modulated signal with a certain bandwidth. After passing through a power divider, two signals are produced. One of these signals serves as the local oscillator for the mixer, while the other is amplified by a power amplifier and then transmitted into free space via an antenna. The transmitted signal encounters a target during its transmission in free space, where a portion is absorbed by the target, and another part is reflected back. This reflected signal is referred to as the echo signal. Subsequently, the echo signal is received by the receiving antenna, amplified by a low noise amplifier, and then inputted into the mixer as the radio frequency input signal. It undergoes mixing operations with the local oscillator signal in the mixer, producing the intermediate frequency signal. Finally, the intermediate frequency signal is filtered and amplified by a Modulator-Analog Converter (ADC), which is then converted into digital data. Through a series of signal processing steps, this digital data can be used to determine the distance and velocity of the target.
Figure 1 FMCW radar architecture
Linear FMCW radar employs frequency modulation methods primarily divided into sawtooth and triangular waveforms. The principles of measurement for FMCW radars using these two types of frequency modulation are fundamentally similar, with the difference being that triangular waveform modulation can simultaneously obtain information on target distance and velocity. However, in multi-target scenarios, it is necessary to perform range-Doppler matching to correctly identify multiple targets; this is more suitable for single-target detection. On the other hand, although sawtooth waveform modulation requires additional signal processing to obtain target distance and velocity information, it does not face the issue of target matching and can be used in complex target detection scenarios. The FMCW radar studied in this paper employs a linear frequency modulation signal based on sawtooth modulation [52], whose frequency modulation characteristics are shown in Figure 2. Each signal cycle (Pulse Repetition Period, PRP) within each signal is referred to as a chirp signal.
